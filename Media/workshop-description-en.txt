[h1]Smart Agriculture, Climate & Colony Logistics[/h1]

Eight independent farming, climate and logistics modules. Every one of them can be switched on or off from the mod settings at any time, mid-game, without restarting — and a module that is off does nothing at all.

You configure it all from one place: [b]select a grow zone and click the new button on its gizmo bar.[/b]

[hr][/hr]
[h1]The modules[/h1]

[h2]1. Field-wide crop rotation[/h2]
[b]The problem:[/b] you switch a zone to a new crop, and because your colonists harvest a cell at a time, the field turns into a checkerboard of half-grown rice next to ripe corn. Nothing ever ripens together again.
[b]What this does:[/b] the zone follows an ordered list of 2 to 4 crops, and only moves to the next one once the last plant of the current crop is gone. Cells harvested early stay bare and wait for the rest of the field. One clean sweep per crop instead of a permanent mess.
Two more kinds of step are available: a [b]fallow[/b] break, which rests the soil and is worth real growth later on (module 8), and [b]random[/b], which rolls a crop that can actually survive — and ideally ripen — in the weather you have when that step comes round.

[h2]2. Stock-triggered farming[/h2]
[b]The problem:[/b] you are sitting on 4000 rice and your farmers keep planting rice.
[b]What this does:[/b] each field watches how much of its own produce the colony holds. Cross the high threshold and it switches to a crop you nominate, or stops sowing entirely. Drop back under the low threshold and it picks up again. Two separate thresholds keep it from flip-flopping every few hours.

[h2]3. Seasonal crop scheduler[/h2]
[b]The problem:[/b] the crop that makes sense in spring is the wrong one in autumn, and remembering to change it by hand is a chore.
[b]What this does:[/b] one tab per season. Give each season a fixed crop, hand it over to the rotation, or leave it fallow. The switch happens on its own as the calendar turns.

[h2]4. True greenhouse climate control[/h2]
[b]The problem:[/b] a room with a glass roof behaves exactly like a room with a stone roof. Sunlight does nothing for the temperature.
[b]What this does:[/b] an enclosed room under a transparent roof warms through the day in proportion to actual sunlight, instead of just tracking the outdoor temperature. Adds a glazing frame to build that roof, and an automated roof vent that opens to dump heat once the room passes a temperature you set. Free winter growing if you build it well — and a real risk of cooking your crops in summer if you do not.

[h2]5. Field harvest crates[/h2]
[b]The problem:[/b] a big harvest leaves produce scattered over a hundred tiles, and your haulers walk it back to the freezer one armful at a time.
[b]What this does:[/b] adds a light, movable crate you place at the field edge. Harvesters drop produce straight into it. Once it fills up — or once the deposits stop, which is what a finished field looks like from the crate — the whole batch is flagged for pickup and taken away together. Underneath it is an ordinary storage building, so vanilla hauling, storage groups and the storage tab all work with it unchanged.

[h2]6. Weather-driven work priorities[/h2]
[b]The problem:[/b] it is 45 degrees outside, or there is toxic fallout, and someone is standing in a field cutting plants until they collapse.
[b]What this does:[/b] while conditions outside are genuinely dangerous — killing heat, a deadly cold snap, toxic fallout and the like — outdoor work types are held back so colonists pick up indoor jobs instead. Growing, plant cutting and mining are covered, and each one can be excluded in the settings.

[h2]7. Pantry first, rot-aware cooking[/h2]
[b]The problem:[/b] cooks always grab the nearest ingredients, so the berries about to spoil sit there spoiling while the fresh rice gets eaten.
[b]What this does:[/b] ingredients are ordered by how long they have left before rotting, closest to spoiling first, with distance only breaking ties. It only kicks in when something in reach is genuinely near spoiling, so cooks are never sent on long walks for no reason.

[h2]8. Soil rest[/h2]
[b]The problem:[/b] leaving a field fallow is what real farmers do, but in RimWorld it buys you absolutely nothing. A fallow step is pure waiting.
[b]What this does:[/b] resting a field banks a fertility reserve in its soil, and the crops that follow spend that reserve by growing faster. The reserve fills to a ceiling worth about as much as rich soil, and empties more slowly than it filled, so one fallow break is spread over several harvests. It is only ever added on top of the terrain's own fertility, never subtracted, so a field can never end up unfarmable. A fallow step can work its own length out — just enough days to fill the reserve — or you can type the number yourself.

[h1]Adding this mod to a game in progress[/h1]

[b]Yes, and nothing needs to be reset.[/b] Load an existing save with the mod enabled and it simply starts working: every field starts with no plan and behaves exactly as it did before, and you enable what you want field by field. The new buildings appear in the build menu straight away. The eight module switches also take effect immediately, mid-game, so you can try one module and turn it off again without ever restarting.

Removing it later is just as safe: no vanilla class is replaced and no vanilla behaviour is skipped or rewritten, so the save still loads. You lose the field plans and any banked soil reserve, and the zones fall back to the crop set on the zone itself. Deconstruct the glazed roof, the roof vent and the crates first, otherwise those tiles come back as plain rubble.

[hr][/hr]
[h1]Compatibility[/h1]

Crop lists are queried from the def database at runtime, so crops from Vanilla Plants Expanded, VGP and anything else appear on their own with no patch needed. No third-party assembly is ever referenced: mods are detected through the loaded mod list and silent def lookups, so nothing breaks when one is absent. Every hook is additive, which is why the mods below coexist with it rather than fight it.

[list]
[*][b]Smart Farming[/b] — compatible, and the two do different jobs: this mod decides what to sow without ever rewriting the crop set on the zone. Both add their own button to a grow zone, so you get both.
[*][b]Vanilla Plants Expanded[/b] and its submodules, [b]VGP[/b], [b]Medieval Overhaul[/b] — compatible. Their crops show up in the crop pickers on their own. One caveat: extra harvest yields that another mod spawns itself land on the ground rather than in a harvest crate.
[*][b]Vanilla Expanded Framework[/b], [b]Common Sense[/b] — compatible. Both sort mixed-ingredient recipes, which module 7 deliberately leaves alone.
[*][b]Hospitality[/b], [b]Hospital[/b] — compatible. Their work-priority changes and module 6 stack, and dangerous weather still wins.
[*][b]LWM's Deep Storage[/b], [b]Adaptive Storage Framework[/b], [b]Pick Up And Haul[/b] — compatible: a harvest crate is an ordinary storage building.
[*][b]Dubs Skylights[/b] and other skylight mods — better than compatible: their roof is given the greenhouse thermal model, and this mod's own glazing frame hides itself so you never see two of everything.
[/list]

No load order requirements beyond Harmony. To check what was detected, the startup log carries a line beginning with [SACL].
